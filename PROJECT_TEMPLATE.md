# Case study worksheet

Use this before adding a public project. Keep notes here until the story is ready; publish only details you can share and defend in an interview.

## 1. One-sentence summary

What problem did you address, what did you do, and what changed?

## 2. Context and constraints

- Who needed the work, and why?
- What made it difficult: scale, timing, fragmented information, adoption, limited access, or another constraint?
- Which details must remain confidential?

## 3. Your contribution

- What decisions did you personally make?
- What did you build, coordinate, improve, or teach?
- Who else contributed? Use accurate wording such as "co-founded," "co-led," or "assisted."
- Which tools or methods mattered, and why were they chosen?

## 4. Outcome and proof

- What changed for users, staff, customers, or the organization?
- Which numbers are measured, estimated, or simply the scale of the work?
- What source supports each claim?
- What would a former colleague confirm about your role?

## 5. Transfer to target roles

Identify two or three capabilities the project demonstrates. Examples: business requirements, workflow design, field operations, resource allocation, low-code automation, quality control, change adoption, or practical AI enablement. Explain the connection in plain language rather than listing tools alone.

## 6. Safe artifact

Can you share a permission-cleared process diagram, anonymized example, template structure, or public link? If not, a clear text case study is enough. Never use actual family addresses, employer records, confidential screenshots, or private documents.

## 7. Final check

- [ ] My contribution is accurately scoped.
- [ ] Implemented work and planned work are labeled separately.
- [ ] Every metric has a defensible source and qualifier.
- [ ] No sensitive or confidential data is included.
- [ ] The page makes sense without reading my resume.
- [ ] The page works on a phone and all links work.
