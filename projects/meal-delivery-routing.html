Routing meal deliveries for 200+ families

During the COVID-19 response, families needed food delivered reliably across a wide service area. I used k-nearest-neighbor proximity logic to help build routes that made geographic sense for volunteer drivers.

Zach Johnson FoundationSenior Program ManagerCOVID-19 response
200+families served
20+drivers coordinated
100,000pounds of food distributed

The challenge

Good routes had to work for people, not just on paper.

Food, volunteer availability, delivery addresses, and time all had to come together. With more than 200 families receiving meals, an unstructured list of stops would make assignments harder to manage and could leave some volunteers with a much longer run than others.

I needed a way to turn addresses into practical route groups while coordinating the broader response: community partnerships, incoming shipments, drivers, and deliveries.

Routing concept

Start with proximity. Check the human workload.

The diagram illustrates the method without showing actual family locations. Nearby stops were considered together, then routes were shaped for volunteer delivery.

My approach

Translate data into assignments volunteers can use.

01

Organize the stops

I used the family address list to identify nearby delivery points with a k-nearest-neighbor approach. That gave me a proximity-based starting point for routes.

02

Shape workable routes

I turned the nearby stops into driver assignments while considering whether volunteers would spend roughly comparable time on delivery.

03

Coordinate the operation

I connected the routing work with partnerships, shipments, and more than 20 volunteer drivers so the plan could be carried out in the field.

The result

A delivery plan that supported scale.

The response distributed 100,000 pounds of food to more than 200 families. Proximity-based routes gave volunteers practical assignments, with delivery time kept roughly comparable across drivers.

Route-time balance is a qualitative observation; no measured efficiency comparison is available.

Where this translates

The same thinking applies beyond meal delivery.

Field service

Group nearby visits while accounting for the capacity and time available to each person.

Operations planning

Turn messy inputs into assignments that a distributed team can understand and execute.

Resource allocation

Use an analytical starting point, then check it against real human constraints before rollout.
